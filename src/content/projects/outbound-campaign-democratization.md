## The problem

Campaign creation was bottlenecked on a handful of operators who had direct API access to the sending tools. Targeting, copy and lead lists lived in disconnected sender platforms with no review trail and no guardrails. Nobody could tell what was going out, to whom, or why, until it had already sent. The goal was self-serve authoring for any rep, without handing out API keys, and with a hard guarantee that nothing would ever send without a human explicitly reviewing and then activating it.

## What I built

I built a "campaign-as-contract" model: every campaign is a YAML definition plus a leads CSV, reviewed like code through a pull request, so the exact targeting, angle, sequence and recipient list are auditable in the diff before anything happens. Behind that sits pure-Python validation and push tooling in `scripts/gtm/`: a strict validator checking schema, manifest and audience guardrails, and per-sender push adapters for Apollo, Lemlist and Smartlead behind a single `push_dispatch.py` router that always creates sequences paused and enrolls leads one at a time with personalization.

On top of that, GitHub Actions workflows (`validate.yml`, `push-on-merge.yml`, `create-campaign.yml`, `notify-campaign-pr.yml`) validate on every push, push the paused sequence on merge, enroll leads, delete the PII-bearing CSV from the repo, and notify the team in Slack. JSON Schemas define the campaign, territory and value-prop contracts with canonical enums and guardrails, and a conversational `campaign-creator` skill resolves territory-scoped audiences via an internal MCP server over Snowflake, with copy generated by a paired `email-copy-crafter` skill. The core safety invariant, enforced end to end: nothing sends until a PR is merged *and* the rep manually activates it in the sender's UI. Two humans, paused by default, always.

## How it works

A rep starts a plain-English conversation with the campaign-creator skill describing who they want to reach and what angle to take. The skill resolves a territory-scoped, capped lead list (rejecting existing customers and anything outside the rep's own territory), generates body-first copy against numeric caps and a banned-phrase list, and writes both out as a campaign contract (a YAML file and a leads CSV) committed as a pull request.

CI then runs the strict validator against schema, manifest and audience guardrails, blocking the merge on any failure. Once CI is green, a CODEOWNERS reviewer inspects targeting, copy and the actual leads CSV before approving. Merging triggers the push-on-merge workflow: the dispatcher routes to the correct sender adapter, creates the sequence in a paused state, enrolls the leads, drops anyone contacted too recently, deletes the leads CSV from the repo, and posts a Slack notification. The sequence sits paused in Apollo, Lemlist or Smartlead until the rep manually flips it on, the only point in the entire pipeline where an email can actually go out.

## Impact

Campaign authoring opened up from a handful of dedicated operators to any rep (AE, AM, marketing or partnerships) with no API access required. What used to be a manual, tool-by-tool setup process now takes a single plain-English conversation and lands as a review-ready PR in minutes. Every campaign now carries full auditability and guardrail enforcement where there was previously none, and the paused-by-default, two-human-gate design has eliminated send-safety and PII risk entirely: zero emails have ever gone out without a human reviewing and then separately activating them.
